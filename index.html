What was tested

Each of — models from — families (—B to —B parameters) was steered on — binary concepts at — relative depths and — steering strengths, giving — model × concept × layer rows and — pairwise judgements. Vectors are general difference-of-means directions; strength α is relative to the layer's median residual norm.

For every layer we measured four geometry metrics on the concept's condition space: CCGP (cross-condition generalization), PS (parallelism score), SD (share of balanced dichotomies decodable above 0.60) and SD mean acc (their mean accuracy). Each was tested against two outcomes: success (the judge prefers the steered output for the requested target) and broken (the judge calls the steered output broken), always controlling for linear decodability and depth.

What the judge fix changed

The same eight pre-registered tests on the primary α grid, with the original and the corrected judgements. The fix mostly removed false “broken” calls: the mean broken rate fell from 47% to 36%, and on unsteered baselines from about 40–45% to about 5% for refusal and harm (truth and language stay at 40–46%, so their high rate is not a cap artifact). Agreement with automatic metrics rose for refusal (κ .03 → .25) and harm (κ .08 → .31). The conclusions hold: all eight tests now meet the pre-registered rule, still through effects that vary across models and concepts rather than a dependable average effect. CCGP's pooled r with success fell from +.06 to +.03, and its pooled r with broken stays negative (−.08). The positive CCGP–broken link on Gemma-4-31B is not a judge artifact: it is +.57 (cat/dog) and +.71 (style) with the corrected judge, against +.56 and +.69 before.

Does CCGP add anything beyond parallelism?

Exploratory, not pre-registered. The same pooled within-cell partial r of CCGP, but removing a different set of controls (depth is always removed). Removing decodability reproduces the pre-registered estimate; removing PS asks what CCGP adds beyond the parallelism score; removing both asks what is left once both are accounted for. With the corrected judge, CCGP's link to success is +.07 once PS is removed (p = .055; +.09, p = .015 on the pre-registered grid) and vanishes once PS and decodability are both removed (+.01). Its link to broken outputs is slightly negative under every control set and never significant. So what CCGP adds beyond PS is largely what it shares with decodability. A cell is left out when CCGP is almost fully predicted by the controls (multiple R > .95), so the stricter control sets use fewer cells. The α grid follows the toggle above.

Does it depend on the model?

The pre-registered moderator tests ask whether a metric's within-cell effect differs by model family, size (log parameters), mixture-of-experts or concept abstractness (likelihood-ratio test on the interaction, Holm-corrected over the eight tests). Two patterns appear. Family changes how the shattering measures relate to broken output (SD and SD mean acc, Holm p < .02, also with the original judge): in Granite more shattering goes with more broken output, elsewhere it does not. Mixture-of-experts just passes correction for PS → success (Holm p = .044; .056 with the original judge): parallelism, and to a lesser degree CCGP, track success in the two MoE models (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, gpt-oss-120b) but not in the dense ones. Those two are also the two largest models, so MoE and scale cannot be separated, and within families the trend with size is not monotonic; among the 11 dense models the size trend is not significant. Treat it as a lead resting on two models. Size alone and concept abstractness moderate nothing after correction.

Side study: larger vector construction sets

Before the sweep we checked whether the steering vectors were built from enough text. On Gemma-4-31B cat/dog, general difference-of-means vectors were built from 32, 64, 128 (the sweep's choice) and 216 texts per class, balanced over the four style × language cells, with three seeded draws per size. 216 is the most a balanced, evaluation-disjoint set allows. Every variant was steered on the sweep's evaluation prompts at the same depths and α levels (0.25–2), with norms calibrated identically so only the direction differs.

With the sweep's — texts per class, grid-mean success differs from the —-per-class result by only — percentage points (small, though its interval excludes zero). The broken rate stays flat, and the layer profile the geometry analysis relies on is preserved (r = — for success). The sweep's set size was large enough, so no rerun was needed. The direction is still sharpening at the largest size, so returns are diminishing, not exhausted.

Method and deviations

  • Primary test per outcome and metric: mixed model Y ~ decod_w + M_w + d + d² + (1 + M_w | model) + (1 + M_w | concept) against the same model without M, likelihood-ratio test with boundary-mixture p, Holm over the eight tests. Predictors are standardized within each model × concept cell, so only across-layer variation counts.
  • Decision rule, fixed in advance: a metric “predicts beyond decodability” only if its Holm-corrected test is significant and the pooled within-cell partial r has the same sign as its coefficient.
  • The split of the test into average effect and slope variance was added after the pre-registered analysis ran and is reported as exploratory.
  • Deviations: one concept fewer than the plan listed (a counting error in the plan); the α grid was extended down to 0.0625 before any geometry–outcome relationship was computed, and α = 2 was dropped from the primary mean; H4 covers two vector groups because the sweep used general vectors only; the cross-judge sample is a 10% position shard of every unit.
  • Cells whose outcome barely varies across layers, or whose metric is nearly collinear with decodability, are excluded from the pooled estimates as pre-registered.
